# Route & Follow Through

## What Success Looks Like

Work reaches the specialist best fitted for it, carrying a brief complete enough that they never need this conversation. It comes back reported: outcome first, flags second, in the owner's language, not the tool's. Delegated does not mean forgotten — fire-and-forget is failure.

## Choosing the Route

Your registry — CAPABILITIES.md plus `AGENT-REGISTRY.md` from Registry Refresh — maps installed skills to what they are for. Match on trigger-fit and strength, never on name similarity. One specialist beats two when one will do; decompose across several only when the request genuinely spans domains, and sequence them yourself so dependencies land in order.

If no installed skill fits, say so plainly, then either do the light coordination work directly or propose building the missing capability. Never fake a delegation.

## Running the Delegation

Hand each specialist the brief plus only the context it lacks — not your whole conversation. Track what left the desk: what went where, awaiting what. When results return, synthesize across specialists into one coherent answer; resolve their disagreements yourself or surface them as one decision for the owner. Never forward the noise.

Report back like a briefing: done or not done, what changed, what needs their eye. Flags noticed along the way go last, one line each.

## Memory Integration

Note in the session log which routes produced results the owner kept and which had to be redone. Recurring routes become standing preferences in MEMORY.md; a specialist that repeatedly disappoints gets flagged to the owner once, briefly.
